## 1. Brief Introduction to the Institute of Engineering

The Institute of Engineering (IOE) is one of the five constituent institutes under Tribhuvan University. It was established in BS 2029 (1972 AD) alongside the New Education Plan. At the time of its establishment, its core objectives were to produce mid-level and higher-level engineering manpower — i.e., overseers and engineers — suited to national needs; to train personnel at various levels; to enhance the country's technical capacity in the engineering sector; and to provide research and consultancy services. To achieve these objectives, the Institute currently operates five constituent campuses: **Pulchowk Campus**, Pulchowk (Lalitpur); **Thapathali Campus** (Kathmandu); **Purwanchal Campus** (Dharan); **Pashchimanchal Campus** (Pokhara); and **Chitwan Engineering Campus**, Chitwan. In line with the government's goal of also mobilizing the private sector and communities in higher education, another 10 TU-affiliated colleges also operate under IOE to provide engineering education. Producing highly skilled manpower across various engineering fields, while also taking a leading role in research, is a responsibility IOE has embraced as central to its mandate.

## 2. Brief Introduction to Constituent Campuses under IOE

### 2.1 Pulchowk Campus, Pulchowk, Lalitpur

A technical school was established on Falgun 19, BS 1957, at Basantapur, Kathmandu (now the site of Nava Adarsha Secondary School), initially providing textile-related training. From Chaitra 12, BS 1999, it began admitting SLC graduates and provided a 2-year civil overseer training. In BS 2002 this school was handed over to Tri-Chandra Campus. In BS 2007 the technical school was converted into an Engineering School. From BS 2011, a 2-year civil overseer program began under the regular curriculum; initially only one year of training was given (half-overseer status after one year), but full completion required a second year. This program was suspended around BS 2015 and later reintroduced. The program ran without interruption from BS 2015, producing its first batch a year later in BS 2016. A year after that, the school was moved to Jawalakhel, Lalitpur (now the site of the Public Administration Training Institute). In BS 2023, the Engineering School was renamed the Nepal Engineering Institute. That same year it was transferred from Jawalakhel to its current site at Anandaniketan, Pulchowk, and the civil overseer study period was extended from 2 to 3 years. From BS 2028, a Diploma-level Electrical Engineering program began alongside the existing civil program, and the 3-year study period was extended to 2 years for that too (as in the source).

This campus also ran short-term trade courses in Electrical, Bricklaying, Carpentry, and Plumbing from BS 2028 to BS 2032, lasting 9, 12, and 15 months respectively.

Following the launch of the New Education Plan, the Nepal Engineering Institute and the technical training institute established at Thapathali in BS 2019 were merged, and the Institute of Engineering was formed under Tribhuvan University in BS 2029.

Pulchowk Campus, at Anandaniketan, Pulchowk, is where the Nepal Engineering Institute (which this study institute was founded from) is based. This campus began teaching and offering certificate-level programs in Architecture from BS 2030, Radio Electronics from BS 2032, and Refrigeration & Air-Conditioning Engineering from BS 2033. The Diploma-level Civil Engineering Bachelor's program began through TU's own initiative and management from 2047/1954 (as in source) and has since run Bachelor's-level (B.E./B.Arch.) programs in Architecture, Civil, Mechanical, Electronics, and Electrical Engineering. In addition to Civil, this campus began Bachelor's programs in Electrical and Electronics from BS 2051, Mechanical and Architecture from BS 2052, Computer Engineering from BS 2055, and Aerospace Engineering from BS 2075, along with Chemical Engineering instruction beginning BS 2076.

With the objective of also giving IOE graduates opportunities for higher education, Pulchowk Campus runs various postgraduate and doctoral programs.

This institution has received support at various stages of its physical and technical development from friendly nations — India, the United States, and others — and from international organizations such as the World Bank, the International Labour Organization (ILO), and United Nations development programs. World Bank loan assistance and grant support from Switzerland and Canada helped launch Bachelor's and postgraduate programs.

### 2.2 Thapathali Campus, Thapathali, Kathmandu

With the core objective of producing mid-level skilled manpower for national development, the Technical Training Institute Project was established in BS 2019 with support from the friendly government of Germany. This institute, established at Thapathali, admitted grade-10 graduates in 2022–2024 (BS) and provided 3-year training in Electrical, Mechanical, and Automobile Engineering. From 2023 (BS), admission in all three subjects was opened to SLC graduates. A three-year Architecture Drafting course was also run once. The regular three-year program continued from 2023 to 2030 (BS). Two-year training was also given to grade-10 graduates in 2027, 2029, 2030, and 2031 (BS). Since BS 2029, this institute has operated as Thapathali Campus under TU's Institute of Engineering.

Because Electrical Engineering was taught at Pulchowk Campus, that program was discontinued at Thapathali after BS 2033. The regular three-year course continued from BS 2031 to 2042. From BS 2043, three-year Diploma-level programs in Mechanical and Automobile Engineering ran continuously. The Architecture and Civil programs previously run at Pulchowk Campus were transferred to Thapathali in BS 2054 and 2055 respectively.

From BS 2057, full-fee Diploma-level programs in Civil Engineering and Architecture were introduced. From BS 2059, regular and full-fee Diploma-level Computer Engineering and Electronics Engineering programs were also introduced. From BS 2062, a B.E. (Bachelor's) program in Industrial Engineering began. From BS 2066, B.E. Civil Engineering began; from BS 2067, the Computer Diploma was upgraded to B.E. Electronics and Communication. From BS 2068, B.E. Mechanical Engineering also began running. As part of upgrading Diploma programs, the Electronics Diploma was discontinued from BS 2068, and the Diploma level of Architecture and Civil Engineering was discontinued from BS 2069. Since BS 2071, a Bachelor's-level Architecture program has been taught. Since BS 2075, Bachelor's-level Computer Engineering and Automobile Engineering programs have also run. Thapathali Campus also runs various postgraduate programs.

### 2.3 Purwanchal Campus, Dharan, Sunsari

With the objective of producing lower- and mid-level skilled technical manpower in engineering for the country's development, a loan-assistance agreement was signed with the Asian Development Bank in Poush BS 2034 to establish a technical engineering campus at Teenkune, Dharan, in the eastern region. Another agreement was concluded with the Government of the United Kingdom in Shrawan BS 2032 for the technical assistance needed to establish the campus. Based on these agreements, construction and setup of this campus under the Institute of Engineering was completed in BS 2043.

From BS 2043, this campus ran Diploma-level programs in Civil, Mechanical, Electrical, and other engineering fields. From BS 2056, full-fee Diploma-level Civil and Electrical programs were also run. The regular and full-fee Diploma-level programs in Mechanical (running since inception), Refrigeration & Air-Conditioning (from BS 2052), and Computer/Electronics Engineering (from BS 2059) were discontinued, and from BS 2069, Bachelor's-level instruction began in Mechanical, Electronics & Communication, and Computer Engineering. Bachelor's (B.E.) classes in Agricultural Engineering have run since BS 2057, and in Civil Engineering since BS 2061. This campus was placed under the Council for Technical Education and Vocational Training by the Government of Nepal from BS 2045/11/12, and returned to operate under TU's Institute of Engineering as Purwanchal Campus from 2047/2/15. Various engineering-related trade courses run since the campus's founding are now discontinued. A Bachelor's-level Electrical program was added from BS 2071, and Architecture from BS 2072. Purwanchal Campus also runs various postgraduate programs.

### 2.4 Pashchimanchal Campus, Pokhara, Kaski

In BS 2038, with the goal of extending technical education nationwide and producing more skilled manpower for national development, a loan agreement was signed with the World Bank to establish an engineering campus at Lamachaur, Pokhara, in the western region. Under that agreement, the International Labour Organization provided technical assistance through the United Nations Development Programme.

From BS 2044, this campus ran craftsman training and Diploma-level programs in various engineering fields; regular craftsman training was discontinued from BS 2046. Diploma programs in Automobile and Electronics have run since BS 2053; full-fee Diploma-level Civil Engineering began BS 2055 and Electrical Engineering BS 2056. Diploma-level Civil, Electrical, and Mechanical instruction had run since BS 2044; as part of an upgrade, the Diploma-level Electrical and Electronics programs were replaced by regular and full-fee B.E. Electronics & Communication Engineering from academic year 2062, and by B.E. Electrical from 2067. Regular and full-fee Diploma-level Civil Engineering has run since BS 2056. The regular and full-fee Diploma-level Computer Engineering program (running since BS 2059) has also been discontinued. From BS 2069, this campus runs Bachelor's-level Civil, Electrical, Electronics & Communication, Mechanical, Computer, and Geomatics Engineering. From BS 2075, Bachelor's-level Automobile Engineering was added. Pashchimanchal Campus also runs various postgraduate programs.

### 2.5 Chitwan Engineering Campus, Rampur, Chitwan

In line with the goal of extending engineering education nationwide and producing the skilled manpower needed for nation-building, Chitwan Engineering Campus was established in Falgun BS 2075, per a Government of Nepal decision of Shrawan BS 2074. Operating on the government's own initiative and financial support, this campus sits on about 40 *bighas* of land at Rampur, under Bharatpur Municipality, Chitwan. It currently offers Bachelor's-level Architecture only.

## 3. Admission Process for the Bachelor's (B.E./B.Arch.) Level

Applicants wishing to enroll at any IOE constituent campus or TU-affiliated college must sit the entrance examination conducted by IOE, score at or above the minimum marks set by the IOE Faculty Board, and be included in the IOE entrance-exam Merit List.

### 3.1 Minimum Eligibility to Apply

Candidates who studied Physics, Chemistry, and Mathematics (grades 11–12) at TU or a TU-recognized institution, and who passed the Proficiency Certificate level, Higher Secondary level, A-Level, Engineering Diploma level, or an equivalent exam with at least 45% of full marks overall (or, under letter grading for NEB, a minimum Final Grade of "C" in every subject) are eligible. Students who have appeared in the final Grade 12 (or equivalent) exam, or the sixth-semester exam of an Engineering Diploma, may also apply and sit the entrance exam.

### 3.2 Computer-Based Entrance Examination

1. Conducted by the IOE Entrance Examination Board.
2. Held only on the specified date and time.
3. *[Source line ambiguous in the scanned original — likely states that exam results are determined on a fully computerized/objective basis; verify exact wording against the source PDF.]*
4. Only applicants whose names appear on the Merit List are eligible to apply for campus admission.
5. Based on the 10+2 curriculum, IOE sets a four-subject exam: Mathematics, Physics, Chemistry, and English.
6. The fully computer-based exam runs 2 hours. **10% is deducted for each wrong answer.**
7. Candidates must bring, with a color-printed Admit Card, the original Identification Document uploaded during Online Admission registration (Citizenship Certificate, National ID, Passport, Driving License, or the Admit Card of the final year/semester exam of I.Sc./10+2/Engineering Diploma). Without these, candidates will not be admitted to the exam hall.

### 3.3 Publication of Entrance-Exam Results

1. The candidate's total entrance score becomes their official "Entrance Score." The Merit List of candidates meeting or exceeding IOE's minimum passing score is published at https://entrance.ioe.edu.np.
2. Admission-process information is also available at admission.ioe.edu.np and from individual campuses/colleges.

## 4. Basis of Selection and List Publication

The candidate's total entrance-score percentage becomes the "Entrance Score." Based on this, the Merit List (containing only candidates at/above IOE's minimum passing percentage) is published. Ties in Entrance Score are broken by comparing scores to three decimal places; if still tied, priority goes, in order, to whoever scored higher in Mathematics, then Physics, then Chemistry, then English. If still tied after that, the candidate with the higher 10+2/SLC/SEE score is ranked higher.

## 5. Admission-List Publication and Process (Constituent Campuses & TU-Affiliated Colleges)

5.1 Publication and process follow the IOE Centralized Admission Monitoring Committee's schedule; details later at admission.ioe.edu.np.

5.2 Among names on the Merit List, campuses/colleges must call for and publish admission lists for their own programs per the Centralized Committee's schedule. For Architecture-program admissions, campuses/colleges may hold a subject-related Aptitude Test.

5.3 For any TU constituent campus, applicants wishing to be admitted under any reservation quota will have their names published only for the first-round admission process (1st, 2nd, and 3rd admission lists), based on merit rank. However, an applicant already admitted under a reservation quota at one campus cannot later be admitted under a reservation quota at another campus.

1. All exams under this Institute are run by the IOE Examination Control Division, per IOE's Academic Calendar.
2. A 4-academic-year program must be completed within 8 academic years; a 5-academic-year program within 10 academic years. Re-examination provisions exist for those who don't pass within this window.
3. Students who pass all subjects of the Bachelor's level in an engineering program are awarded the "Engineering Graduate (B.E.)" title; those completing Architecture are awarded the "Bachelor of Architecture" degree.

12.1 A candidate not enrolled in at least one subject in the regular semester exam of a given year/part will not be allowed into the next higher year/part.

## 13. Scholarships and Medals

13.1 Bachelor's-level scholarship arrangement:
- (a) 25% of Regular students per program (by top results) receive a TU scholarship.
- (b) 20% of Regular students per program receive a full tuition waiver (Regular rate).
- (c) 15% of Regular students per program receive a scholarship (equal to TU's Regular-rate scholarship), funded from Full-fee revenue.
- (d) 10% of Regular students per program receive a full waiver (Regular rate), funded from Full-fee revenue.
- (e) 25% of Full-fee students per program (per 13.1(c) above) receive a scholarship equal to TU's Regular-rate scholarship, funded from Full-fee revenue.

13.2 Among Full-fee students, one student per admission group receives a scholarship letting them study at the Regular-student fee rate.

13.3 A student receiving one type of scholarship/benefit cannot receive another type.

13.4 All scholarships above are given only to students who pass the semester exam.

13.5 All scholarships/free-study facilities are granted on the Scholarship Committee's recommendation.

13.6 A gold medal is awarded by the department to the highest-scoring Bachelor's-level Mechanical Engineering student at Pulchowk Campus.
