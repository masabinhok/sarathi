01 · Problem statementReport §1.2

The information is published.
It is not usable.

Several thousand candidates sit the IOE entrance examination each year. Everything they need exists — and none of it is answerable as written.

  • Scattered

    Six independent notice boards, each publishing on its own schedule.

  • Not in English

    Largely Nepali, dated in Bikram Sambat, distributed as scanned PDFs that cannot be searched.

  • The gap gets
    filled anyway

    General assistants are consulted here and answer confidently and often wrongly — because a deadline, a fee, a merit rank or a seat count is exactly what a language model is least able to recall.

  • And the cost
    is asymmetric

    A wrong answer about a Python function costs minutes. A wrong answer about a submission deadline costs an academic year.

02 · ObjectivesReport §1.3

What we set out to build.

General objective — to design, implement and evaluate a retrieval-augmented conversational assistant giving accurate, source-attributed and calendar-aware answers on the IOE entrance examination and the admission process that follows it, operating entirely on locally hosted models.

  • Specific · 1

    Assemble and translate a corpus of official notices, index it in a persistent vector store, and implement retrieval and generation as an explicit state graph whose stages are separately addressable.

  • Specific · 2

    Answer exact-value questions — pass-list membership, fee totals, programme cut-offs, BS date arithmetic — by lookup and computation rather than generation.

  • Specific · 3

    Attribute every answer to the documents it visibly used, and decline what the notices do not cover.

  • Specific · 4

    Deliver it as a responsive web interface with streaming answers, an aggregated notice feed and an extracted deadline list — and evaluate it against the failure modes it was built to remove.

03 · ScopeReport §1.5

One cycle, one institution,
answered properly.

The core — conversational retrieval

BE and BArch entrance and admission, IOE, for the 2083 BS (2026) cycle.

Eligibility · application procedure · corrections · examination structure and syllabus · fee payment through four electronic channels · quota documentation, domestic and foreign · result publication · campus and programme structure · deadlines · declaration of campus priorities.

This is what the 13 indexed notices answer, and it is the project's first responsibility.

Four capabilities beyond retrieval

Exact pass-list lookup — by form number, merit rank, name or district.

Deterministic fee computation — every total a fee question can require, four categories.

Historical priority analysis — last-admitted rank per programme, Pulchowk.

Aggregated notice feed — listings from the official boards, both calendars.

04 · Corpus and ingestionReport §3.6, §4.4.1

Fifteen scanned PDFs in.
216 passages out.

Official PDFscanned, Nepali Markdowntranslated, front matter Header splith1 / h2 / h3 Size splits=1200, o=150 Chromabge-m3, HNSW cosine
Prepared by hand, on purpose

Translated semantically, not word-for-word. Nepali terms kept in parentheses on first use. Devanagari numerals converted; BS dates left unconverted, so the conversion machinery resolves them rather than the translator.

Every document records its own translation type, so a reader can tell reproduced text from translated text. The original PDF stays authoritative.

And two that are not prose

The pass list (7,179 rows) and the Pulchowk priority applications (1,700 rows) are transcribed to CSV — embedding a table is useless.

Both are excluded from the vector index by directory convention and read only by the exact-lookup modules.

05 · How retrieval worksReport §4.4.2

Over-fetch, demote, then floor.

Three deliberate choices between the question and the six passages that reach the prompt.

  • Over-fetch
    2k for k = 6

    Request twice the target so a demoted passage can be genuinely displaced by a better-suited one, not merely reordered among those already chosen.

  • Audience demotion
    λ = 0.10

    Foreign-applicant documents are penalised unless the query signals a foreign applicant. Chosen against measurement: competing passages on a quota question separate by ≈0.03, so λ reliably reorders — yet stays small enough that a foreign-only document still surfaces when nothing else matches.

  • Relevance floor
    τ = 0.45

    Set well below the on-topic band. Its job is to exclude passages when a question misses the corpus entirely — not to arbitrate among plausible ones. Measured separation: ≈0.6 on-topic against ≈0.3 off-topic.

Notice listings are written into the same collection, keyed by URL, so one retrieval covers both the corpus and the live feed.

07 · The organising principleReport §1.4, §6.1

Every quantity a student may act upon
is computed, never generated.

The consequential questions in this domain are not retrieval problems. They have single correct answers, and nearest-neighbour search does not produce them.

  • Pass list

    7,179 rows answered by exact lookup — form number, rank, name or district.

  • Fees

    Summed from published line items and checked against the printed totals before they ever reach the model.

  • Dates

    Bikram Sambat converted before the prompt is assembled, so the model never does calendar arithmetic.

  • Cut-offs

    Reconstructed by simulating the published priority applications under the serial-dictatorship rule IOE actually applies.

The settled result is handed to the model as authoritative context. Its job is the sentence around the figure — never the figure.

10 · Challenges and solutionsReport §4.7.1–4.7.2

In every case the symptom
was not the cause.

  • The system prompt
    was being discarded

    Symptom — prompts reported as long and ineffective; rules from twenty-three issues having no effect; invented programmes.
    Cause — no context window was specified, so the runtime served 4,096 tokens against a grounded prompt of 5,318. Overflow is discarded silently and the instruction, being first, goes first. Confirmed directly: an 8,040-token probe evaluated 2,050 and answered from the filler.
    Fix — one constant, 16,384, applied to every model client in the process.

  • Answers in the
    wrong language

    Symptom — asked for Nepali, replied in a Hindi–Nepali mixture; asked in Nepali, invented syllabus content.
    Cause — the rule lived in the prompt. Four formulations were tried and each failed differently. Naming the request is what keeps it alive.
    Fix — the request is not named, it is removed — stripped from the question, with the app emitting its own fixed sentence. 0/4 → 24/24.

10 · Challenges and solutionsReport §4.7.3–4.7.4
  • Out-of-scope answers
    corrupting the thread

    Symptom — reported as two defects: a holiday question answered with a planning questionnaire, and then apparent memory loss.
    Cause — one defect, not two. Memory was intact. Once the questionnaire is in the transcript, the follow-up "bahamas" is retrieved against as though it were an IOE question and every turn after it is incoherent.
    Fix — scope became a node of the graph, not a sentence in a prompt. Relevance alone cannot decide it — the distributions overlap.

  • Arithmetic performed
    inside the model

    Symptom — asked the eight-semester total it multiplied and trailed off; asked for a total it invented a per-programme tuition table appearing in no document; asked what is refundable it invented a refund policy.
    Cause — retrieval was correct. The arithmetic was the failure — across three tables, one of which is a part of the total rather than an addition to it.
    Fix — every total a fee question can ask for is precomputed and verified. The model reads a line; it never adds one.

The finding that matters

Reliability was obtained from the pipeline, not from the model.

No improvement above came from changing the generator. Scope rose through a change of framing; English enforcement improved by deleting an instruction rather than strengthening one; and the single most consequential defect was that the instruction had been silently discarded all along.

12 · LimitationsReport §1.6, §5.2.2

Stated plainly.

What the results do not establish

Corpus currency. A snapshot of the 2083 cycle. A notice published after indexing is visible only as a listing — the assistant can say one exists, not what it says.

Translation dependency. Retrieval runs over English translations. A translation error propagates to every answer from that passage; the Nepali PDF remains authoritative.

Evaluation scale. Curated probe sets of tens of questions, sized to the failure modes investigated. No figure here is a population-level accuracy estimate.

And the honest rest

Model capacity. Seven billion parameters, chosen to fit commodity hardware. Several design decisions exist solely to compensate.

Coverage of computation. Fees and priority analysis cover Pulchowk, whose schedule alone is published in full. Cut-offs move each year — guidance for ordering preferences, never a prediction.

No authentication. Conversations are scoped to a browser identifier. This separates histories; it is not a security boundary.

13 · Conclusions and future workReport §6

Three conclusions.

  • The corpus problem
    is tractable

    Thirteen notices, translated once with provenance recorded, index to 216 passages that answer this domain. Next year's cycle is replacing documents and rebuilding the index — no retraining, no code change.

  • The consequential
    questions are not
    retrieval problems

    They have single correct answers. Exact lookup and deterministic computation produce correctness that can be verified — all twenty fee totals, all sixteen cut-off pairs.

  • Reliability came from
    the pipeline

    Not from the model. Not one improvement reported came from changing the generator.

Future work

Extend fee and priority coverage to the remaining campuses · fetch and extract notice bodies, not only listings · a user study across a live admission cycle.
