01 · The problemSarathi

Everything a student needs is published.
None of it is in one place.

A candidate who has just passed the entrance exam has days to act, and the information is spread across sites that do not know about each other.

  • Seven sites

    Two IOE boards and five campuses, each publishing its own notices on its own schedule.

  • Two scripts

    Notices arrive in Nepali and English — often in the same document.

  • Two calendars

    Deadlines printed in Bikram Sambat; the student's phone shows Gregorian.

  • One wrong move

    Decline a lower priority you were offered, and you are out of the admission process entirely.

02 · What Sarathi isSarathi

A question answered from the notices,
with the notice attached.

It answers

Fees — worked totals, not a table to read.

Seats — every campus, every programme.

Your result — by form number, rank, name or district.

Your chances — against four published years of cutoffs.

It runs

Entirely on one machine. qwen2.5:7b generates, bge-m3 embeds, both through Ollama on a single 8 GB GPU.

No API key, no cloud inference, no per-student cost — and no question about a candidate's result ever leaves the building.

05 · The decision everything hangs offSarathi

Tools select. The application places.

The model chooses which evidence a question needs. It has no say in where that evidence sits in the prompt.

Why it matters

With a 7B model, whatever sits nearest the question wins. Tool results arriving as messages would be ordered by call order — which is to say, by the model.

So the app re-renders every result into its own ordered blocks. The worked fee figures go last, nearest the question.

What it cost to learn

Placed ahead of the retrieved documents, the fee block was ignored: the model went back to the raw fee tables in the retrieved text and multiplied them itself.

The answer was confident, well-formatted, and wrong. Issue 23 in the project log is that failure and its five rounds of layout work.

06 · The deterministic floorSarathi

The model may add tools.
It may not take any away.

Regexes that predate the rewrite still decide the minimum evidence a question gets. The model's choices are additive on top.

  • ensure_default_calls

    Guarantees the tool is called — retrieval always, plus the pass list, fees, seats or cutoffs whenever their detectors fire.

  • enforce_floor

    Guarantees the block arrives. Calling a tool turned out not to be the same as getting its answer.

  • Why both exist

    The planner emitted category: "full_fee" against a strict enum. Schema validation failed, the call errored, and nine cases lost their figures while every other tool in the same batch succeeded — 49/53 → 44/53.

Total tool-selection failure now degrades to exactly the previous pipeline, plus a wasted half-second.

Minor Project Defense8
07 · Grounded, not generatedSarathi

The numbers check themselves.

Every module that holds figures stores line items only, derives every total, and asserts the result against what the notice printed.

Self-verifying data

seats.verify() — 17 derived totals against the booklet's own subtotals and grand total.

cutoffs.verify() — all 228 rows, two ways. Every programme with a cutoff must have seats at that campus in a table transcribed by a separate route; every Full-fee cutoff must sit deeper than its Regular twin.

And when it cannot answer

Retrieval hands over its documents before the first token is written, so a citation is a record of what was read — not something the model composed.

When nothing matches, the app says so. An honest "the published materials do not cover this" is a correct answer; a plausible invented one is the failure this project exists to prevent.

09 · Three things measurement foundSarathi

Each one overturned the obvious answer.

  • The prompt was
    never read

    num_ctx was unset, so Ollama served 4,096 tokens. A real turn assembles 5,318. The overflow is discarded silently, from the front — which is where the system prompt sits. Proved directly: an 8,040-token prompt evaluated 2,050 and answered from the filler.

  • A regex that could
    never match

    Five Devanagari fee terms sat inside \b(…)\b. A Devanagari word ends in a combining vowel sign, which Python does not count as a word character — so धरौती was not unreliable, it was unreachable.

  • Never an
    OCR problem

    13 of 15 source PDFs have a real text layer. One is annotated in our own corpus as "a scanned PDF with no text layer" — it has 14,827 characters of one, in a legacy font that maps Devanagari onto ASCII. It read as rubble, so it was retyped by hand.

10 · LimitationsStated, not discovered

What it does not do.

The weak-evidence gap · 2 of 66

An off-topic question can still find a plausible-looking document and be answered instead of declined.

Measured rather than assumed: off-topic questions score 0.357–0.576 against the corpus, genuine ones 0.562–0.701. The distributions overlap — the worst off-topic outscores two real questions.

No threshold separates them. It needs a different mechanism, and raising the cutoff would start refusing real students.

And the honest rest

A 7B model on consumer hardware has a quality ceiling — it is why so much is computed in code rather than asked of it.

Priority allocation is Pulchowk only, one year. Cutoffs are four campuses, open category, first list.

No user study yet. Every claim here is about the system, not about students using it.
